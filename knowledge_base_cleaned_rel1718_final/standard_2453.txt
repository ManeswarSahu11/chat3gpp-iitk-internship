Title Path: Scope
Content: The present document describes and defines performance metrics for popular multimedia services in 3G networks, including packed-switched streaming service (PSS), multimedia broadcast multicast service (MBMS), video telephony (VT), and IP multimedia subsystem service (IMS). The present document has a top-down approach, which starts with the Quality of Experience (QoE) parameters and metrics, and then provides End-to-end Service QoS (ESQoS) and System Quality of Service (SQoS) parameters and metrics and mapping between these different layers.

Title Path: General description and approach > Definitions
Content: Quality of Experience (QoE) The overall acceptability of an application or service, as perceived subjectively by the end-user (see notes 1 and 2). [NOTE 1:]Quality of Experience includes the complete end-to-end system effects (client, terminal, network, services infrastructure, etc). [NOTE 2:]Overall acceptability may be influenced by user expectations and context. For the purpose of the present document, QoE is limited to those parameters that are reasonably expected to be under the control of the Service Provider, i.e. it excludes aspects of specific terminal equipment implementation. In the document, the exact E2E measurement point will be defined for each service. QoE indicates performance metrics as expressed from the end service user's point of view. They can be required or reported by the common users, and may be stated irrespective of their measurability. SA4 has defined Quality of Experience (QoE) metrics and their transport for PSS and MBMS in and , respectively. The PSS and MBMS QoE metrics features are optional for both PSS and MBMS streaming server and clients, and shall not disturb the PSS and MBMS service. A PSS or MBMS client supporting the feature shall perform the quality measurements in accordance to the measurement definitions, aggregate them into client QoE metrics and report the metrics to the PSS or MBMS server using described procedures in and for PSS and MBMS, respectively. The way the QoE metrics are processed and made available is out of the scope of the present document. In general, a Service Provider will set service requirements in line with the end-user's expected QoE, which needs to be translated into parameters or metrics that the service provider can control or measure. Thus, it is necessary to map QoE metrics to measurable End-to-end Service QoS and System QoS parameters, which provide the means for the service providers to guarantee the service quality. End-to-end Service QoS (ESQoS) ESQoS is generally used to specify the performance of services from the perspective of operators and service providers. As the number of new services proliferates and becomes more complex, it is important for an operator to measure a network's ESQoS accurately and continuously improve it to achieve customer loyalty and maintain competitive edge. ESQoS is measurable. It can be quantified exactly by several digital parameters, unlike QoE. System Quality of Service (SQoS) The concept of SQoS is a subset of conventional QoS, which is defined in ITU-T Recommendation E.800 as the collective effect of service performances which together determines the satisfaction of a user of a service. It is characterized by the combined aspects of performance factors applicable to all services, such as: service support performance; service operability performance; service accessibility performance; service retainability performance; service integrity performance; service security performance; Compared with ESQoS, SQoS denotes the point-to-point QoS, which is specifically related to the units and links of network systems, rather than the whole service or a network. SQoS can be viewed as the QoS consideration from the viewpoint of network operators.

Title Path: General description and approach > SQoS parameters
Content: The quality of a service perceived by the user perceives is influenced by the network as well as by the terminal equipment. Within the present document, the influence of the terminal equipment and application have not been included. Services are applications running on the common network components. Title Path: \% probability;
Content: mean; target value; Least Acceptable Value (LAV). This forms the basis of candidate material for standardization, and must be aligned with the work of the ITU-T.
Title Path: \% probability; > Service non-access
Content: The service cannot be accessed by the UE when requested by the user. The service non-access may be caused by the network unavailability or shutdown of service server, etc.
Title Path: \% probability; > Service failure
Content: The service can be accessed, but something occurs once the service is in use resulting in failure of service for the user. The service failure may be caused by overload of server, handoff of user or network congestion, etc.
Title Path: \% probability; > Service setting-up time
Content: The service setting-up time is the period from the time of service request to the time of service playing. The service setting-up time may vary from microsecond to seconds, and depends on the service requested by the user.
Title Path: \% probability; > Re-buffering
Content: Re-buffering denotes the time and the frequency of re-buffering during the usage of service. The main reason of re-buffering is the data network transmission rate cannot keep up with the real-time play requirements of the user's terminal.
Title Path: \% probability; > Image corruption
Content: Image corruption refers to the degree of corruption quality of a single image.
Title Path: \% probability; > Edge noise
Content: Edge Noise denotes the form of edge busyness that is characterized by spatially varying distortion that occurs in close proximity to the edges of objects in a video display.
Title Path: \% probability; > Blurriness
Content: Blurriness denotes the image/video quality as being indistinct.
Title Path: \% probability; > Colour reproduction accuracy
Content: Colour reproduction denotes the quality of accuracy in colour reproduction procedure.
Title Path: \% probability; > Blockiness
Content: Blockiness refers to the degree of image distortions that occurs in DCT-block edge compression due to compression error or packet loss.
Title Path: \% probability; > Incontinuous image with block
Content: Incontinuous image with block denotes the degree of data block consistency among continuous frames.
Title Path: \% probability; > Freeze image
Content: Freeze image refers to the degree of frozen image when video is playing, which is caused by inadequate received data or low frame transmission rate.
Title Path: \% probability; > Audio quality
Content: Audio quality describes the quality of audio as perceived by the user and mainly is decided by the codec algorithm, network transmission delay and capacity of terminal.
Title Path: \% probability; > Audio/Video synchronization error
Content: Audio/video synchronization error describes the time difference of the audio and video signal at the user side. Audio/video synchronization error is mainly caused by the network transmission delay and buffering delay.

Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer
Content: SQoS parameters are defined according to one of three layers (Application, Transport and sub-Transport) as well as the network structure, SQoS parameters are first classified according to the layers. Some parameters are selected according to protocol layers and give the definitions of each parameter. Most of the definitions are cited from documents of standards.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Application > Frame rate
Content: Definition: The playing rate of frames, i.e. 30 frame/sec denotes a playing of 30 frames in one second.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Application > Frame loss ratio
Content: Definition: Frame loss ratio indicates the extent of frame loss between the sender and receiver. Frame loss ratio=number of the unreceived frames / number of all sending frames.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Application > Frame error ratio
Content: Definition: Frame error ratio=number of all error frame received/ number of all frames sent.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Application > Transaction rate
Content: Definition: The average rate for servers to transact the data packet. Those servers may include: SGSN, GGSN, VLR, HLR, AuC, web server, media server, etc.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Application > Transaction delay
Content: Definition: The sum of the time for a data packet to wait in queue and receive the service during the server transaction.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Transport > Bandwidth
Content: Definition: The quantity of data transmitted per unit time in the network, usually expressed as bps.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Transport > Transfer delay
Content: Definition: dT, the transfer delay from Src to Dst at time T is defined by the statement: Src sent the first bit of a unit data to Dst at wire-time T and that Dst received the last bit of that packet at wire-time T+dT. NOTE: If the transfer delay from Src to Dst at T is undefined (informally, infinite), this means that Src sent the first bit of a unit data to Dst at wire-time T and that Dst did not receive that unit data .
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Transport > Jitter
Content: Definition: The differential time between the packet actual arrival time and its expected arrival time according to a standard clock.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Transport > Packet loss ratio
Content: Definition: Packet loss ratio=the number of unreceived packets/the number of packets sent.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Transport > Packet error ratio
Content: Definition: Packet error ratio the number of received frames that contain an error in the frame payload divided by the total number of transmitted frames.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Frame loss ratio
Content: Definition: Frame loss ratio= the number of unreceived frames/the number of all sending frames.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Residual frame error ratio
Content: Definition: The residual frame error ratio is the number of undetected error frames divided by the total number of transmitted frames in one direction of a single virtual connection.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Frame discard ratio
Content: Definition: The number of received frames that are discarded because of a frame error divided by the total number of transmitted frames in one direction of a single virtual connection. Frame errors are defined as follows: Frames those are too long or too short; Frames that are not a multiple of 8 bits in length; Frames with an invalid or unrecognized DLCI; Frames with an abort sequence; Frames with improper flag delimitation; 6) Frames that fail FCS .
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Residual bit ratio
Content: Definition: Indicates the undetected bit error ratio in the delivered SDUs. If no error detection is requested, Residual bit error ratio indicates the bit error ratio in the delivered SDUs .
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Access delay
Content: Definition: The delay time in queue of the station waiting for base station to assign a channel.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Block ratio
Content: Definition: The frequency of channel arrangement request failure.
Title Path: SQoS parameters > SQoS parameters of each layer > Sub-transport > Soft handoff rate
Content: Definition: The average rate for clients to handoff from one district to another.
